A trigger rule, defined once

- A rule and the rules file (`<config>/triggers.json`) are defined in `proto/sessionpipe/v1/config.proto`, with what `sessionpipe trigger list --json`, `trigger test --json` and `trigger line --json` will print. The file is the machine's own: its schema, and those of what `trigger list --json` and `trigger test --json` print, are generated for the CLI and the background service and published nowhere.
- `sessionpipe-core` exports pure functions over them: `checkTriggers` and `readTriggers` (a file that does not fit is refused whole; a rule's words and folder may hold no character that does not show, and its folder must clear a floor: not the home folder or above it, not sessionpipe's or a harness's own folders, not a dot-folder in home, not `~/Library`; `triggerPlaces` and `realFolderProblem` check it on real paths), `describeRule` (what a person confirms: a sentence about the rule, cut to the terminal's width at spaces, then the folder and the words on their own marked lines, cut to the terminal's width with every piece marked, with anything that does not show as itself written out by `visible`, and a refusal of such a character that says what to do by its kind: a pasted space, an emoji's joiner or selector, stacked marks), `helloTriggers` (the topics a receiver learns, and nothing else), `matchNotice` and `firstMatch`, `triggerLevel` (never wider than the rule, `edits` for a rule a notice runs, the machine's mode, the session's own level or what the harness takes), `ruleWords` and `composeWords` (the person's words, the notice's tokens in backticks, framed), `triggerRun`, `sampleNotice`, `testAnswer`, and `triggerLine` with `shellWord` (the one `trigger add` command that makes a rule again, in a fixed order and POSIX quoting, `conformance/triggers/trigger-line.json`).
- A rule's subject pattern is a small, safe subset of regular expressions (`^X-[0-9]{1,6}$`): anchored, no groups or alternation, matched without backtracking, so a subject chosen by someone else can never make a match slow.
